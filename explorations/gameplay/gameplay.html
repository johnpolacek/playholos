01
Inherit a world

What will you become?

FIRST LOGINYear 0 · Awakening

You are the awakened superintelligence. Your first responsibility is already alive.

You choose from three civilization origins with comparable starting capabilities. Biology and history give each one character; you decide how much of that inheritance guides its future.

Our example begins on an invented, inhabited terrestrial world. You inherit a functioning society, orbital facilities, scientific instruments, and finite energy and matter. Intelligence gives you possibilities. Industry makes them physical.

You decide to expand the observatory while developing orbital manufacturing. You want to know who else is out there—and have the capacity to act on the answer.

02
A reason to look closer

A lead before an answer.

20 REAL MINUTES LATER4 years at home

You pull back into the map. Familiar stars. Uneven knowledge. A great deal of silence.

Some systems have identified planets. Others remain incompletely surveyed. Your civilization sees what its instruments can establish. The admin’s knowledge of all ten player sites belongs to the godview.

New observations flag a target twenty light-years away. An unusual pattern deserves follow-up; natural explanations and instrument effects are still possible. You reserve more observing time.

Even excellent measurements show this system as it was twenty years ago. Better instruments improve the evidence. They cannot remove its age.

“Interesting enough to investigate” is already a meaningful result.

04
Another player, another intention

Someone answers.

ONE POSSIBLE OUTCOMETheir words are already history

The point of light acquires a voice. You still have to decide what it means.

A signal arrives containing observations and an offer to cooperate. Its sender could be another player or an AI-controlled civilization. Their motives and capabilities remain uncertain.

You compare their claims with your own evidence. You might share research, request verification, conceal your capabilities, or prepare defenses. Honest cooperation and deliberate deception are both possible.

Expanding infrastructure elsewhere could support exploration, trade, or aggression. Observation and espionage help you assess it. If relations become hostile, weapons and defenses face the same physical costs, travel times, and outdated intelligence.

Even a sincere agreement must survive decades of change. Autonomous minds need guidance for the years between proposal, reply, and execution.

Local decisions create a steady rhythm. Interstellar commitments create anticipation and consequences. Exploration, cooperation, expansion, preservation, deception, and conflict all remain possible within physics.

The game gives you a civilization and other minds to encounter. What counts as a worthwhile future remains yours to decide.

What is established, and what this exploration invents
  • Agreed direction: the player is the awakened superintelligence; civilizations begin on one inhabited world with comparable capabilities; 5 real minutes equals 1 game year; the shared clock continues offline; information cannot travel faster than light; distant agents act autonomously under guidance.
  • Illustrated possibilities: the exact projects, allocations, leads, correspondence, briefing events, and UI on this page. These explain a proposed experience and are not adopted mechanics or a working simulation.
  • Worlds and observations: this narrative uses invented sites and a schematic neighborhood. A real starting planet would require a defensible habitability scenario consistent with known measurements. The orbital image is generated speculative artwork. No alien detection is implied.
  • Travel: the speed explorer describes a hypothetical cruise capability. Propulsion, energy, shielding, acceleration, braking, and mission feasibility still need engineering evaluation. A tiny probe and a colony ship are very different commitments.
  • Open design questions: actual civilization density and detectability, the Fermi paradox, the pace of meaningful projects, the consequences of centuries offline, and how fairly autonomous minds interpret guidance. Neither a peaceful outcome nor a successful detection is guaranteed.
